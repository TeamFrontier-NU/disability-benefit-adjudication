## 3. What the team's research contains

**`SSI_SSDI_Forms/`**: blank official SSA forms downloaded 2026-10-23, with a README that maps them to the pitch scope:

- SSA-16-BK (SSDI application), SSA-8000-BK (SSI application)
- SSA-3368-BK (Adult Disability Report): the core input, covering conditions, providers, medicines, work
- SSA-827 (authorization to release records), SSA-3369 (Work History Report), SSA-3373-BK (Function Report)
- The README's advice: build the prototype on SSA-3368 plus made-up medical records, and add SSA-16 or SSA-8000 by claim type.

**`SOAR sample/`**: a complete public SOAR training packet for a fictional claimant, "Annette Farnsworth" (placeholder SSN 222-22-2222, "Any Town, YY"), filed 01/17/2017. It contains:

- Initial-claim documents (in our scope): SOAR checklist, SSA-8000, SSA-16, SSA-3368 (filled), SSA-827, SSA-3288, and a 6-page Medical Summary Report (MSR) co-signed by a case manager and a psychiatrist
- Representative forms: SSA-1696, SSA-1699
- Appeal-stage forms (out of our scope): SSA-561 reconsideration, SSA-3441 appeal report, HA-501 hearing request

Why it matters for us:

1. It shows what a real, filled initial claim looks like field by field (100 of 460 fields filled on the SSA-3368; 132 of 1,114 on the SSA-8000). This is the template our 50 made-up cases should copy.
2. It's a natural adversarial case. The claimant self-reports leukemia, cerebral palsy, polio and tumors that no provider has confirmed, while the confirmed diagnosis is schizophrenia. A tool that counts self-report would get this wrong. That links directly to feedback #2.
3. It has internal inconsistencies: the MSR is dated January 17, 2017 but describes appointments in March and April 2017. Real files have errors like this, and our cases should too.
4. It shows a document type our pitch doesn't mention: the SOAR MSR, a narrative written by a non-medical case worker. Our rules need to say how much weight it gets (see follow-up 02, threat T4).

## 6. Update, 2026-10-24 (later): design finalized and platform started

The team chose the product shape: a platform that imports existing files (and later links to the government's claim system), sorts each claim with AI, lets examiners review and decide, and logs the final decision. Accuracy and speed numbers come from internal tests on simulated data, since no government data is available.

It includes the examiner panel, import connectors (with a planned connector for SSA's claim folder), the Claude reader plus an offline baseline, the rule table and routing, a decision log with timing, and metrics.
